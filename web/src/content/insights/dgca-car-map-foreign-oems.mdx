Start with eGCA registration, not the Civil Aviation Requirements themselves — you cannot productively map your product against a CAR until your entity is registered and DGCA has a case reference to attach the mapping to.

## Where do you actually start — eGCA or the CARs?

DGCA's eGCA platform [PLACEHOLDER: verify current eGCA portal URL] is the entry point for most regulatory interactions with the Directorate General of Civil Aviation, including applications that eventually touch a specific Civil Aviation Requirement. Registering the entity correctly here — the right applicant type, the right point of contact, the right product category — is what determines whether the subsequent CAR application moves or stalls. Foreign OEMs who go straight to a CAR application without a clean eGCA registration in place tend to lose weeks to administrative correction rather than substantive review.

## Which CAR actually applies to your product?

It depends entirely on what you are bringing into the market, and this is the step that most needs an experienced hand rather than a generic checklist. As a rough map: CAR-145 governs maintenance, repair and overhaul organisations; CAR-M covers continuing airworthiness management; and CAR-21 covers design and production organisations and the certification of aeronautical products, parts and appliances. A ground-service technology, an aircraft system retrofit, and a seating product each land in a different place in that structure, and DGCA has revised more than 100 CARs in the past six months [PLACEHOLDER: confirm current CAR revision count and dates at time of publication], so a mapping done even a year ago should be re-checked against the current text before you rely on it.

## Is there a faster route through an FAA-DGCA bilateral arrangement?

For some products, yes — where an FAA-DGCA Implementation Procedures for Airworthiness (IPA) arrangement applies, an existing FAA approval can shorten the Indian validation process rather than requiring a full independent review [PLACEHOLDER: confirm current scope and product coverage of the FAA-DGCA IPA]. This is worth checking early, because it changes both the timeline and the evidence package you need to assemble — but it is not a substitute for the eGCA registration and CAR mapping steps, only a way to move through them faster once your product's category is confirmed.

## What does "field representation" actually mean in practice, once you are approved?

Approval gets a product into the market; it does not put someone in the room with your Indian airline or MRO clientele day to day. Field representation is the ongoing, on-the-ground presence — attending programme meetings, fielding operational questions, and being the local point of contact — that turns a regulatory approval into an actual commercial relationship. Stelia Aerospace, a manufacturer of business-class aircraft seats for airlines worldwide, is served this way in India: the approval work and the field representation are separate but connected functions, and a foreign OEM usually needs both.

## What does OEM-to-OEM coordination add, beyond the regulator?

Where your product interacts with an airframe or an aircraft system, the airframer's own approval can matter as much as the regulator's — and running both tracks in parallel rather than sequentially is usually faster than it sounds. TaxiBot's Indian market entry required Airbus and Boeing approvals running alongside the DGCA compliance case at the same time, on the reasoning that a single-type clearance would not have changed any airline's decision to adopt the system. The same logic applies to most multi-airframe products: sequencing the airframer conversations after the regulatory ones (or vice versa) usually just adds time without adding safety.
