WORK / TAXIBOT INDIA
KSU Aviation Pvt. Ltd. · 2019–2024 · DEL, BLR, HYD

Bringing the world’s first TaxiBot operation into service

Semi-robotic aircraft dispatch towing, certified with two airframers and cleared by the regulator — in a market where neither had happened before.

Client
KSU Aviation Pvt. Ltd.
Airports
Delhi · Bangalore · Hyderabad
Aircraft cleared
A321 family · B737‑NG
Our role
Market entry, approvals, evaluation
Challenge

A product with no regulatory precedent anywhere

TaxiBot moves an aircraft from stand to runway on the nose gear, under the pilot’s control, with the main engines off. The fuel and emissions case is obvious. The approval case was not: dispatch towing at taxi speed puts loads through a nose landing gear that no airframer had cleared for it, and no civil aviation authority had a rule to apply.

So the work was never really about selling equipment. It was about getting two airframers and one regulator to agree on something none of them had a precedent for — and then proving it on a live apron without disrupting a single departure.

Air India A321 · Indira Gandhi International, Delhi · 2022
GoAir A320 · night dispatch trial · 2021
Approach
01

Build the market case first

Fuel burn, emissions and stand-occupancy modelling per carrier and per airport, so the commercial argument existed before the certification argument was asked for.

02

Take the regulator through it, not around it

Documented the operation as a procedure rather than a product: crew responsibilities, speed and turn limits, coupling and decoupling, abnormal handling. Compliance evidence assembled to the standard an auditor would ask for, because that is the discipline the practice is built on.

03

Two airframers, in parallel

Ran the Airbus and Boeing approval tracks at the same time rather than sequentially, on the argument that a single-type clearance would not change any airline’s ground fleet decision.

04

Prove it in revenue service

Operational evaluation on live departures across three airports, including night operations, with fallback to conventional pushback available at every stage.

Outcome

World’s first TaxiBot operations with Airbus (A321 family) and Boeing (B737‑NG).

Both airframer clearances obtained, regulatory compliance established, and the system delivered into scheduled service at three Indian airports. India became the reference market other operators cite when they ask their own regulator the same question.
